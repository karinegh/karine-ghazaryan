The story

I build platforms that turn audience data into actionable decisions.

I've spent my career in data-first companies where owning the data isn't enough — you have to analyze it, find the patterns, and turn them into recommendations that drive action. That's shaped how I think about every product I build: the output isn't a dashboard, it's a decision.

What makes me different: I came up through design, advertising, and paid media before moving into product — so I understand what marketing and research teams actually need, not just what engineers can build. 10+ years across market research SaaS, AdTech, and consumer intelligence. Six platforms shipped.

Act 01
Building order
from chaos
2019 — Pre-COVID

I joined Fuel Cycle as the only product manager — walking into a pure firefighting culture. No Agile, no engineering–product process, no roadmap culture. Everything reactive. I built the product-led organization from the ground up — workflows, Agile development, prioritization systems, cross-functional operating model. Then I turned my attention to the platform itself: a powerful but deeply complex community research tool built exclusively for professional market researchers. I democratized it — expanding the target user from market researchers to UX teams, product managers, HR, and marketing — making research a capability every function could run independently.

Act 02
Growing during
COVID
2020 — 2021

While SaaS companies bled revenue, Fuel Cycle grew. We had a native video conversation tool embedded inside the platform — purpose-built for market research, where interviews and focus group recordings fed directly into insights and reporting without any tool-stitching. When remote work became mandatory and researchers couldn't conduct in-person studies, we were already there. I partnered with sales to aggressively pitch this capability to new departments and buyers. The result: highest platform engagement in company history, upsells into new business units, and client retention near 98%. Simultaneously, I used this window to lead the architectural shift of breaking the monolith into microservices — the structural foundation that made everything that followed possible.

98% client retention

Act 03
Expanding who
research serves
Post-COVID · New categories

Post-COVID, I introduced an entirely new user category: product beta testers. Working with Philips, I enabled their teams to run real product testing — smart bulbs, physical hardware — through the platform. Testers submitted structured feedback that flowed directly into Jira, landing in engineering and product teams as actionable tickets. Same model with Amazon Alexa and Sonos. This wasn't just a new use case — it required building multi-level user management and permissioning into the platform architecture, and opened a completely new enterprise buyer: the product team, not just the research team.

Philips product testing
Amazon Alexa
Sonos · Carhartt
Jira integration layer
Multi-tier user management
Act 04
A new era
for research
The Research Engine · AI · Autonomous Insights

The final act was the biggest: a completely new platform era. I led the architecture of the Research Engine — solving three problems at once. First, templated research automation: plug-and-play methodology templates so anyone could design and launch studies without a research agency — cutting weeks of setup to hours. Second, audience flexibility beyond community: researchers could now run studies inside their community, distribute a link anywhere, or purchase a targeted audience through the Fuel Cycle Audience Network — and compare all three side by side, quant and qual in one place. Third, the AI layer: moving from manual analysis to autonomous insights — AI that proactively detects research gaps, initiates studies, synthesizes unstructured data, and delivers branded reports and executive summaries automatically. What I built became what Fuel Cycle calls today their Autonomous Insights platform — 85% faster, 10× cheaper, 5× more studies per quarter.
